# Ideal Customer Profiles

This document defines who Qeetro should serve first, who it should serve later, and who it should reject early.

The strategic rule is simple: win software execution before expanding into broader work management.

## ICP Sequence

Qeetro should pursue customers in this order:

1. AI-native startups and scale-ups.
2. Mid-market software organizations with Jira or Linear pain.
3. Enterprise software organizations with AI governance and execution visibility needs.
4. Adjacent technical functions inside existing customers.
5. Broader enterprise work management only if it strengthens the execution substrate.

Skipping this sequence creates product bloat and go-to-market confusion.

## 2026 Buyer Context

Public competitor research shows that buyers will not experience Qeetro as "the AI tool in a market without AI." By 2026, many alternatives already market AI teammates, agents, enterprise search, connected context, automated prioritization, and workflow automation.

The early buyer pain is therefore sharper:

- They have AI tools, but not a governed execution substrate.
- They have agents in code, chat, docs, or work tools, but no coherent operating model for agent identity, permissions, audit, and accountability.
- They have connected search, but not reliable state inference across software execution.
- They have flexible work platforms, but flexibility has created cognitive load and semantic ambiguity.
- They have developer tools with AI, but product, roadmap, design, docs, customer feedback, and dependency context remain fragmented.

The best early customers will already believe AI belongs in execution workflows. Qeetro's job is to give that belief an operating system.

## Tier 1 ICP: AI-Native Software Teams

### Profile

Companies:

- AI-native SaaS, developer tools, infrastructure, fintech, vertical SaaS, cybersecurity, data, platform, and product-led software companies.
- 20 to 1,000 employees.
- 10 to 500 engineers.
- Seed through Series C, with early public-company potential later.
- Engineering-led culture.
- Modern stack, usually GitHub or GitLab, Slack, Notion or Google Docs, Linear or GitHub Projects, CI/CD, cloud hosting, and modern observability.

### Why They Matter

These customers are the fastest route to product love and category credibility. They are already dissatisfied with old work-management assumptions, already using AI, and already willing to adopt new software if it respects their time.

### Current Pain

- Linear is strong and agent-forward, but customers may need deeper governance, broader cross-tool execution context, and stronger multi-team visibility.
- GitHub and GitLab are close to code, but product intent, roadmap, docs, design, customer feedback, and cross-functional coordination still live elsewhere.
- Notion is useful for docs and increasingly AI-assisted, but flexibility becomes fragile as execution workflows scale.
- Plane may appeal to self-hosted and developer-extensible buyers, but some teams will prefer a sharper hosted execution system with stronger category focus.
- Jira is culturally rejected by many modern software teams, even when enterprise buyers respect its footprint.
- Status updates and sprint rituals consume manager time.
- AI is used across tools, but governance and execution context are fragmented.
- Cross-team dependencies are discovered too late.
- Roadmaps are detached from delivery reality.

### Buying Triggers

- Engineering team grows past 30, 75, or 150 engineers.
- A new VP Engineering, CTO, or Head of Product audits the toolchain.
- Linear or GitHub Projects becomes insufficient for roadmap or dependency visibility.
- Agent usage grows faster than internal governance.
- A failed AI workflow exposes missing permission, audit, or approval boundaries.
- A missed launch exposes coordination failure.
- Leadership declares an AI-native operating model.
- A peer company publicly adopts Qeetro.

### Buyer Map

- Economic buyer: CTO, VP Engineering, Head of Product, or technical founder.
- Champion: engineering manager, product lead, staff engineer, engineering ops lead.
- Influencers: developers, PMs, designers, QA, DevOps.
- Blockers: security review, finance, existing tool owner.

### Required Product Proof

- Time to first value under 5 minutes.
- Issue and project workflows feel faster than current tools.
- GitHub or GitLab integration is immediately useful.
- AI summaries and search save visible time in the first week.
- AI outputs cite evidence and respect permissions.
- Agent actions are inspectable, even if early autonomy is limited.
- Import from current tools is credible.
- Developers do not feel surveilled or burdened.
- The product is opinionated without being rigid.

### Success Metrics

- Activation within the first session.
- Team-level adoption within 14 days.
- Weekly retention from engineers and PMs.
- Reduction in manual status update work.
- Expansion from one team to multiple teams.
- Strong qualitative advocacy from technical users.

## Tier 2 ICP: Mid-Market Software Organizations

### Profile

- 500 to 5,000 employees.
- 200 to 2,000 engineers.
- Established SaaS, fintech, marketplace, developer platform, data, security, or vertical software organizations.
- Jira footprint with pockets of Linear, GitHub Projects, Notion, or spreadsheets.
- Formal security and procurement, but still engineering-led enough to adopt modern tools.

### Current Pain

- Jira configuration debt and performance frustration.
- Toolchain fragmentation between Jira, Confluence, Slack, GitHub, spreadsheets, and BI dashboards.
- Engineering leaders lack reliable cross-team visibility.
- Managers spend too much time translating work state into executive updates.
- AI adoption is unmanaged and inconsistent.
- Portfolio planning is brittle.

### Buying Triggers

- Toolchain consolidation initiative.
- Jira renewal pressure.
- AI governance initiative.
- New engineering productivity function.
- Reorganization or scaling event.
- Failed delivery milestone caused by coordination breakdown.

### Required Product Proof

- Enterprise-grade identity, RBAC, audit, and tenant isolation.
- Migration path from Jira or Linear.
- Multi-team roadmap and dependency visibility.
- Useful executive summaries with traceable evidence.
- Reliable integrations with GitHub, GitLab, Slack, and docs sources.
- Clear AI governance model.

### Success Metrics

- Successful pilot conversion.
- Multi-team expansion.
- Reduction in manual reporting time.
- Replacement or consolidation of at least one incumbent workflow.
- Security and procurement approval without major custom work.

## Tier 3 ICP: Enterprise Software Organizations

### Profile

- 5,000+ employees.
- 1,000+ engineers.
- Large SaaS, financial services, retail technology, healthcare technology, telecom, automotive software, and other software-heavy enterprises.
- Strong compliance, procurement, security, and governance needs.
- Existing Jira or Azure DevOps footprint.

### Current Pain

- High cost of maintaining Jira and related tools.
- Dedicated admin teams managing configuration complexity.
- Fragmented execution visibility across divisions.
- AI usage growing faster than governance.
- Leadership wants productivity gains but cannot trust ungoverned AI.
- Tooling feels hostile to modern engineering talent.

### Buying Triggers

- CIO or CTO mandate to modernize engineering systems.
- AI governance program.
- Major delivery failure.
- Atlassian pricing or migration pressure.
- M&A integration requiring toolchain consolidation.
- Board-level engineering productivity initiative.

### Required Product Proof

- Enterprise security and compliance maturity.
- SSO, SCIM, RBAC, audit logs, retention, data export, and tenant isolation.
- High-scale performance.
- Migration tooling and professional migration patterns.
- Advanced admin controls that do not slow daily users.
- Agent governance with approval, audit, and policy controls.

### Success Metrics

- Division-level pilot succeeds.
- Migration risk is reduced enough for phased rollout.
- Executive sponsor can show measurable coordination cost reduction.
- Security team approves AI and data handling.
- Expansion path is clear before the first contract closes.

## Anti-ICP

Qeetro should reject or defer:

- ITSM-first organizations wanting change boards, CAB processes, and ITIL depth.
- Marketing or operations teams seeking a generic work OS.
- Customers whose primary need is custom fields, custom statuses, conditional transitions, and workflow scripting.
- Solopreneurs and freelancers as a strategic segment.
- Government and defense buyers before compliance readiness is real.
- Buyers hostile to AI-assisted workflows.
- Customers that require heavy implementation services before first value.
- Prospects buying only on lowest price.

## Qualification Rubric

Score each dimension from 0 to 2. A strong early ICP fit is 11 or higher out of 16.

| Dimension               | 0                                | 1                      | 2                                           |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Engineering-led culture | Non-technical buyer dominates    | Mixed                  | Technical leadership owns decision          |
| Current tool pain       | Mild                             | Some fragmentation     | Acute Jira, Linear, or GitHub Projects pain |
| AI posture              | Skeptical                        | Exploring              | Actively becoming AI-native                 |
| Team size               | Too small or too large for phase | Adjacent               | Squarely in target range                    |
| Buying trigger          | None                             | Soft                   | Active and time-bound                       |
| Champion                | None                             | Individual contributor | Manager or above with influence             |
| Developer influence     | Low                              | Moderate               | High                                        |
| Expansion path          | Unclear                          | Possible               | Obvious multi-team expansion                |

## Strategic ICP Rule

The best customer is not the largest customer. The best early customer is the one whose pain, culture, urgency, and influence make Qeetro sharper.

Qeetro should choose customers that pull the product toward speed, AI-native execution, developer trust, and coherent workflows. It should reject customers that pull the product toward admin-heavy configuration.
